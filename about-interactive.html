Research Philosophy & Core Mission

My research sits at the productive tension between building things that work perfectly — and understanding exactly how they fail.

I'm a Lecturer and Researcher at the School of Computer Science and Information Technology, Adelaide University. My work focuses on the co-development, empirical evaluation, and implementation of AI-driven, self-adaptive, and cyber-resilient software systems at the intersection of Cyber Security, Software Engineering, and Generative AI.

Mission

Pursue multidisciplinary, industry-driven research with solid theoretical foundations — from empirical software engineering and vulnerability mitigation to AI-assisted secure code generation and automated resilience in critical systems.

Vision

Transform next-generation AI and software security from reactive patching into proactive, self-healing, trustworthy computing ecosystems — empowering organizations to harness Generative AI without compromising their digital infrastructure.

Defence Innovation & Tactical Cyber Resilience

Working at the edge of autonomous systems and national security — where software failures have real-world consequences.

Defense research is unforgiving. The systems I work on cannot afford downtime, cannot wait for a human to respond, and cannot expose their own vulnerabilities. My two major defense contributions:

Adaptive Dynamic Application Orchestrator — A$250,000

Project Manager for a Defence Trailblazer & DEWC Services initiative. We're building an AI-driven orchestration platform that dynamically constructs and deploys mission-critical tactical applications in contested, communications-denied environments.

Command-and-Control Cyber Resilience — A$422,000

With DSTG, Cisco, and Cyber Security CRC, I simulated sophisticated multi-stage cyber-attacks on military C2 networks, identified cascading vulnerability paths, and designed self-adaptive isolation protocols that respond within milliseconds.

AI Ventures with Human Impact

The most sophisticated algorithm is meaningless unless it makes someone's life meaningfully better.

Over two years in Australia's startup ecosystem, I brought machine learning into contexts where the stakes were deeply personal:

Kindship (NDIS Provider)

Designed and deployed a machine-learning recommendation engine to connect families of differently abled children with precisely matched therapists — eliminating weeks of frustrating manual searching and improving therapy outcomes through better initial matches.

Migrova (Stone & Chalk / Antler)

As Chief Project Officer, secured a A$100,000 Seed-Start grant from the SA Department of State Development. Led the architectural design and deployment of AI-powered matching infrastructure to help international migrants navigate complex relocation processes.

Both ventures reinforced a conviction I carry into every research project: when you design for the most constrained users — those with the least margin for error — you tend to build genuinely better technology for everyone.

Where It All Started: High-Precision Industrial Engineering

My engineering instincts didn't begin with code. They started with machines, operators, and the discipline of zero-defect operations.

At Tetra Pak, I led 20+ technicians across continuous 24/7 shifts on industrial laminator lines — contributing to Industry 4.0 automation initiatives and managing output KPIs under constant operational pressure. At Prolife Foods in Australia, I supervised production quality for roaster operations, translating SOPs into real-time operational decisions.

What these experiences gave me that formal education couldn't: a visceral understanding of what system failure actually costs, not in abstract metrics, but in halted production, disrupted teams, and missed targets. That understanding became the lens through which I now approach every software system I design or secure.

Tetra Pak Pakistan (2017–2019)

Team lead for 20+ operators on high-speed laminator lines. Led automation initiatives aligned with Industry 4.0 principles, managing shift KPIs and continuous improvement cycles.

Prolife Foods Australia (2020)

Supervised roaster machine operations in compliance with Standard Operating Procedures, maintaining quality assurance standards across multi-batch production runs.
